DEPOSITION OF JENNA M. MORROW, R.N.
Estate of Lorraine Pettibone v. St. Calloway Regional Medical Center
Taken June 12, 2025

                                                      1
 1                     EXAMINATION
 2       BY MR. ACKROYD:
 3       Q.  Ms. Morrow, would you state your name for the
 4       record, please.
 5       A.  Jenna Marie Morrow.
 6       Q.  And you're a registered nurse?
 7       A.  Yes, I've been an RN for about eleven years now,
 8       the last seven at St. Calloway, almost all of that on
 9       4 West, which is the medical-surgical floor, so mostly
10       post-op patients and some diabetic patients and so on.
11       Q.  Were you working the night of September 9, 2024?
12       A.  I was. I worked seven p.m. to seven a.m., it was
13       my third night in a row, which I remember because I'd
14       swapped with someone.
15       Q.  Was Lorraine Pettibone one of your patients that
16       night?
17       A.  Yes, she was one of my five. Room 412, the bed by
18       the window.
19       Q.  What was she being treated for?
20       A.  She'd come in for a hip fracture repair, but she
21       was also a diabetic, so she was on a sliding-scale
22       insulin order, meaning you check her sugar and you
23       give a dose based on the number.
24       Q.  How many times did you give her insulin during
25       that shift?
26       A.  Four times, I think. Well, I pulled insulin for
27       her four times, I'm sure about that, because I went
28       back through it with risk management afterward.

                                                      2
 1       Q.  Let's focus on the dose you gave around ten
 2       o'clock. What time exactly?
 3       A.  I gave it at 9:52. That's what I charted and
 4       that's what the scanner shows.
 5       Q.  Before you gave that dose, did you look at the
 6       order?
 7       A.  Of course. I looked at it on the screen on the
 8       workstation outside her room.
 9       Q.  What did the order say?
10       A.  The order on the screen said forty units. I
11       remember thinking that's a lot for her, because her
12       earlier doses had been small.
13       Q.  What did you do about that?
14       A.  I called the pharmacy. I called before I gave it,
15       at about 9:30, and I spoke to Dr. Sethi himself, and
16       he told me the dose was fine.
17       Q.  How do you know it was Dr. Sethi?
18       A.  He said his name when he answered, "Pharmacy,
19       Sethi," the way he always does, and I've worked with
20       him for years so I know his voice.
21       Q.  Did you document that call?
22       A.  No, and I know I should have. It was a busy night,
23       we were short a tech.
24       Q.  Where did you get the insulin from?
25       A.  From the automated dispensing cabinet in the med
26       room, her pocket in the refrigerated drawer.
27       Q.  Describe the vial you took.
28       A.  The vial I pulled from her pocket was the U-500,

                                                      3
 1       the concentrated one, with the striped label. That's
 2       the only thing that was in there.
 3       Q.  Did you recognize it as concentrated insulin at
 4       the time?
 5       A.  Honestly, not in the moment. The striped label, I
 6       see that now, but I was looking at the patient's name
 7       on the bag sticker, and it was her name.
 8            MS. LIU:  Objection. Move to strike everything
 9       after "not in the moment" as nonresponsive.
10            MR. ACKROYD:  You can make that motion at trial,
11       counsel.
12       Q.  What did you draw up?
13       A.  Forty units on an insulin syringe. That's what the
14       order said.
15       Q.  What happened afterward?
16       A.  About an hour later her aide found her clammy and
17       hard to rouse, and I checked her sugar and it was 38,
18       so I called a rapid response.
19       Q.  Did you learn anything later about what was in
20       that vial?
21       A.  Risk management told me it was U-500, which, I
22       mean, I knew by then.
