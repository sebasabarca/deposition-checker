 2                NAKASHIMA v. CORVEL MEDICAL DEVICES, INC.

 4              DEPOSITION OF GRACE OBUYA - February 25, 2026
 5        GRACE OBUYA, having been duly sworn, testified as follows:
 6                               EXAMINATION
 7    BY MS. RAMIREZ-COLE:
 8    Q.   Ms. Obuya, where do you work?
 9    A.   Corvel Medical Devices, in Building 3. I'm a quality
10    engineer, it'll be six years in May, and for most of that time
11    I've also been the team lead for the sterilization techs on day
12    shift, which is kind of an unofficial thing, but it's in my review
13    so I guess it's official.
14    Q.   Did you work with Kenji Nakashima?
15    A.   Every day, pretty much. He was one of my techs. Good tech,
16    careful, he was the one people went to when the chamber printer
17    jammed.
18    Q.   Were you present at a meeting on July 14, 2025, with Mr.
19    Nakashima and Darren Holloway?
20    A.   Yes. In 2B, the little conference room by the break room. It
21    was in the afternoon, two o'clock. I was his lead, so Rick asked
22    me to sit in because he was out that week.
23    Q.   Were you there for the whole meeting?
24    A.   Start to finish. I walked in with Kenji and I walked out with
25    Kenji. It was maybe twenty minutes.
26    Q.   What was discussed?
27    A.   Darren talked about his training hours, that he was behind on
28    a couple of the online modules, and something about clocking in

                                                                     4
 1    late a few times in June. It was, honestly, it felt like a routine
 2    check-in.
 3    Q.   Was any document given to Mr. Nakashima in that meeting?
 4         MR. ASHCROFT:  Objection, form.
 5    A.   Nobody handed Kenji anything in that meeting. There wasn't
 6    any paper on the table at all, Darren had his laptop and that was
 7    it. I remember because afterward Kenji asked me if he was supposed
 8    to get something in writing and I said I didn't know.
 9    Q.   Did you see Mr. Nakashima leave with any document?
10    A.   No. He left with his coffee mug. That's all he had.
11    Q.   Let's turn to September 9, 2025. Was there a meeting that day
12    about the sterilization load records?
13    A.   Yes, at ten in the morning, in 2B again. Kenji had found load
14    records where the cycle times written on the record didn't match
15    the chamber printouts, and he asked for the meeting.
16    Q.   Who attended?
17    A.   Kenji, me, Rick Sandoval, and Darren Holloway.
18    Q.   Are you sure Mr. Holloway was there?
19         MR. ASHCROFT:  Objection, asked and answered.
20    A.   Yes. Darren Holloway was there. He sat at the far end by the
21    window and he was typing notes on his laptop the whole time. He
22    asked Kenji whether he'd told anyone outside the company.
23    Q.   How long did the meeting last?
24    A.   Forty-five minutes, maybe a little less.
25    Q.   Was anyone else there?
26    A.   No. Just the four of us.
27    Q.   What procedure governs the load records?
28    A.   SOP-114.

 1    Q.   What did it require as to signatures?
 2    A.   One signature. The procedure only needed the operator's
 3    signature. I trained people on it, so I know it pretty well. The
 4    reviewer box was on the form but it was optional, that's what I
 5    always told people.
 6    Q.   Do you know which revision of SOP-114 was in effect in
 7    September 2025?
 8    A.   I couldn't tell you the letter. It gets revised every year or
 9    so, and I'll be honest, I don't always catch every change.
10    Q.   After September 9, did anything change in how Mr. Nakashima
11    was treated?
12         MR. ASHCROFT:  Objection, form.
13    A.   He felt like it did. He said Rick stopped talking to him in
14    the hallway. I didn't see anything myself, I mean I'm not, I
15    didn't see anything I'd call different.
16    Q.   When did you learn that Mr. Nakashima had been terminated?
17    A.   October 3. I came in around seven-forty and he was at the
18    front entrance with a security guy. His badge didn't work at the
19    front door that morning. He'd tried it a couple of times, he told
20    me.
21    Q.   Did you speak with him later?
22    A.   That evening, by phone. He called me.
23    Q.   What did he tell you?
24         MR. ASHCROFT:  Objection, hearsay.
25         MS. RAMIREZ-COLE:  Not offered for the truth. You can answer.
26    A.   That they let him go. He told me he got a severance paper and
27    had three weeks to think about it. He wanted to know if he should
28    sign, and I said I couldn't give him advice on that.
