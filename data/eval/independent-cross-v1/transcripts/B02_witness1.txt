 1                      SUPERIOR COURT, CARROW COUNTY
 2    NADINE LINDEN, et al., Plaintiffs, v. SAFFRON TABLE LLC, Defendant.
 3                          Case No. CV-2025-1182
 4             DEPOSITION OF PRIYA WENDELL - November 18, 2025
 5               PRIYA WENDELL, sworn, testified as follows:
 6                               EXAMINATION
 7    BY MR. OYELARAN:
 8    Q.   Ms. Wendell, what is your position with Saffron Table?
 9    A.   Kitchen manager. I have held that position since March 2023.
10    Q.   What are your responsibilities?
11    A.   Ordering, receiving, back-of-house scheduling, the food
12    safety logs, and staff training on our HACCP plan.
13    Q.   Do you hold any food safety certification?
14    A.   Yes. A certified food protection manager credential, renewed
15    in January 2025.
16    Q.   How many cooks worked in the kitchen in June 2025?
17    A.   Five line cooks and two prep cooks, in addition to me.
18    Q.   I want to talk about the chicken salad served at lunch on
19    Friday, June 6, 2025. When was that chicken salad prepared?
20    A.   Thursday, June 5, at approximately two p.m.
21    Q.   How many batches of chicken salad were made that week?
22    A.   One. A single eight-quart batch on Thursday. We did not make
23    chicken salad on any other day that week.
24    Q.   Who prepared it?
25    A.   Marco Tellez made it. I was standing at the prep table across
26    from him while he mixed it. I watched him fold in the mayonnaise
27    and the celery.
28    Q.   Why were you watching?

                                                                     7
 1    A.   He was relatively new to that recipe, and I wanted to confirm
 2    he followed the cooling and portioning steps.
 3    Q.   Where was it stored after it was made?
 4    A.   In the walk-in cooler, on the second shelf, in two covered
 5    hotel pans with labels dated six-five.
 6    Q.   Let me ask about the walk-in cooler. Who opened the kitchen
 7    on Friday, June 6?
 8    A.   Marco and I opened together. We both arrived at six
 9    twenty-five.
10    Q.   Did you check the walk-in temperature that morning?
11    A.   Yes. It is the first thing I do. At six-thirty the dial
12    inside the walk-in door read 38 degrees.
13    Q.   Is that dial the only thermometer in the walk-in?
14    A.   It was at that time. There was no digital probe in the
15    walk-in in June.
16        (Wendell Exhibit 7 was marked for identification.)
17    Q.   Exhibit 7 is a document titled Cooler Temperature Log, June
18    2025. Is that your handwriting on the June 6 line?
19    A.   Yes. "6:30, 38, PW." Those are my initials.
20    Q.   Did anyone else look at the walk-in dial at six-thirty?
21         MS. FAIRCLOTH:  Objection, calls for speculation.
22    A.   Marco was in the walk-in with me pulling produce. I can't say
23    what he looked at.
24    Q.   Let's talk about sanitizer. What sanitizer does the kitchen
25    use?
26    A.   A quaternary ammonium sanitizer, dispensed from a wall unit.
27    The target concentration is two hundred parts per million.
28    Q.   How is the concentration checked?

                                                                     8
 1    A.   With quat test strips. You dip the strip for ten seconds and
 2    compare it with the color chart on the vial.
 3    Q.   What did the strips show during the week of June 2?
 4    A.   The sanitizer tested at two hundred parts per million. I test
 5    the buckets at the start of every shift I work, and they were at
 6    two hundred.
 7    Q.   Every bucket?
 8    A.   The buckets at the prep station and on the line. I don't test
 9    the bar.
10    Q.   When did the restaurant first learn of guests becoming ill?
11    A.   Friday evening, around seven, a guest called to say her
12    husband was sick after lunch.
13    Q.   How many guests ultimately reported illness?
14    A.   Thirty-one in total. Twenty-four had contacted us by the end
15    of Friday night, and seven more contacted us over the weekend.
16    Q.   Was there an inspection afterward?
17    A.   Yes. The county environmental health department inspected on
18    Monday, June 9.
19    Q.   Did the county issue a written report?
20    A.   Yes. The county's report listed four violations.
21    Q.   What were they?
22    A.   The walk-in door gasket, a missing thermometer in the
23    reach-in on the line, date labels on a sauce container, and a hand
24    sink blocked by a cart.
25    Q.   Was any other inspection done?
26    A.   Corporate's food safety team did an internal audit on June
27    11. I was not given a copy of that report.
28    Q.   Did the county take samples?

                                                                     9
 1    A.   Yes, of the chicken salad remaining in the walk-in and of the
 2    mayonnaise. I don't have the lab results; those went to the owner.
 3    Q.   Have you made any changes to the kitchen procedures since
 4    June?
 5         MS. FAIRCLOTH:  Objection. Subsequent remedial measures. I'll
 6    let her answer for discovery purposes.
 7    A.   We installed a digital probe with an alarm in the walk-in in
 8    July, and we now log temperatures twice a day instead of once.
 9    Q.   Was Marco Tellez disciplined?
10    A.   No. Nobody was disciplined.
11    Q.   Is he still employed there?
12    A.   No. He left in August for another job.
13         MR. OYELARAN:  I have nothing further at this time.
14         MS. FAIRCLOTH:  No questions. We'll read and sign.
15        (Deposition concluded at 10:47 a.m.)
