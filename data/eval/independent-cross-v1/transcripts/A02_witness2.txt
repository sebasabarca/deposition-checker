Deposition of Arvind Sethi, PharmD
Estate of Lorraine Pettibone v. St. Calloway Regional Medical Center
June 19, 2025

EXAMINATION BY MR. ACKROYD
Q: Please state your name.
A: Arvind Sethi.
Q: What is your profession?
A: I'm a clinical pharmacist. I hold a PharmD. I've been at St.
Calloway, I believe, about nine years.
Q: Where did you train?
A: Pharmacy school at Wexford State, and then a one-year residency
in hospital pharmacy. I think that was 2015, possibly 2016.
Q: Are you licensed in this state?
A: Yes. My license is current, as far as I know.
Q: Were you on duty on the night of September 9, 2024?
A: I was. I was the overnight pharmacist, the only one in the main
pharmacy from 7:00 p.m. to 7:30 a.m., if I'm remembering the
schedule correctly.
Q: What were your duties that night?
A: Verifying new orders, mostly. Answering calls from the floors.
Overseeing the technician who restocks the dispensing cabinets.
Q: Did you verify an insulin order for Lorraine Pettibone that
night?
A: I verified one order for her that evening, yes.
Q: What did that order say?
A: The order I verified read four units. Four units of regular
insulin, subcutaneous. That's my recollection, and I believe the
verification log would bear that out.
Q: Was the order changed after you verified it?
A: There were, as I understand it, several revisions of that order
that evening, by different prescribers. I can only speak to the
version that came into my queue. I don't know what the nurse's
screen showed at any given time.
Q: Did Nurse Jenna Morrow call you about Mrs. Pettibone's insulin
that night?
A: No. Ms. Morrow did not call me that night. I received no call
from 4 West about Mrs. Pettibone's insulin, before or after the
dose.
Q: How can you be sure?
A: Because I would have remembered a question about a forty-unit
dose for an elderly patient. And the pharmacy phone log shows no
call from 4 West between 9:00 and 10:30.
Q: Were you the only person answering the pharmacy line?
A: After 9:00 p.m., yes. The technician doesn't take clinical calls.
Q: Let's talk about the dispensing cabinet on 4 West. What insulin
was stocked in Mrs. Pettibone's pocket that night?
A: Her pocket in the refrigerated drawer on 4 West contained only
U-100 vials that night. Regular insulin, U-100. There was no U-500
in her pocket.
Q: How do you know that?
A: I reviewed the cabinet restock report. The technician restocked
her pocket at 8:40 p.m. with two vials of U-100, and nothing else
was loaded into that pocket before the dose. I also, I should say, I
looked at the restock scans myself the next morning.
Q: Where is U-500 stored at St. Calloway?
A: It's supposed to be stored only in the main pharmacy, in a
separate bin, and dispensed patient-specific. That's the policy.
Whether that was followed on every occasion, I can't say.
Q: How many times did the cabinet record insulin withdrawals for
Mrs. Pettibone that night?
A: The system logged six insulin withdrawals for her from that
cabinet during the night shift. Two of the six were cancelled
transactions, where the drawer opened and the nurse cancelled
without removing anything. So, four actual removals.
(Exhibit 14 was marked for identification.)
Q: Dr. Sethi, is Exhibit 14 the cabinet transaction report you just
described?
A: It appears to be. Yes, this is the format our system prints.
Q: Do you have personal knowledge of when the dose was actually
given?
A: Not personally. I was told by the night house supervisor that the
dose went in just before ten.
Q: Did you go to 4 West that night?
A: I was paged to the rapid response, I believe around 11:00, to
bring dextrose. I brought it up myself.
Q: Did you see Ms. Morrow?
A: I saw her in the room. We didn't really speak. It was, it was a
busy room.
Q: Has the hospital changed its insulin storage practices since
September 2024?
MS. LIU: Objection, subsequent remedial measures. You can answer.
A: I believe U-500 is now dispensed only in pens with a different
color cap, but I'm not on the committee that decided that.
Q: Have you been disciplined in connection with this incident?
A: No. I was interviewed by risk management, twice I think, but no
discipline.
Q: Thank you, Doctor. Nothing further.
MS. LIU: No questions from me.
(Whereupon the deposition concluded at 11:40 a.m.)
